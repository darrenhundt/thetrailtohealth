# Craft Short-Form Video Hooks

Treat every hook as a testable hypothesis, never a guarantee of reach. Optimize for qualified attention and honest payoff, not empty clicks.

## Gather the brief

Obtain or infer:

- platform and format: talking head, vlog, montage, text-led, demonstration, or other;
- intended viewer, their current state, and the language they naturally use;
- topic, core promise, and the first body beat that fulfills it;
- available proof: experience, demonstration, result, source, number, or example;
- brand voice, prohibited language, accessibility needs, and sensitive-topic constraints;
- desired action and available performance baseline.

Ask only for missing information that would materially change the result. Flag assumptions.

## Define the promise

Write one sentence stating what the viewer will receive after the opening. Reject any hook that the body cannot begin paying off immediately.

Treat the hook as the opening 3–5 seconds across spoken words, on-screen text, and visuals. It earns continued attention; it does not need to summarize the entire video.

## Mine precedents

When references or browsing are available:

1. Match format first, then use the same or an adjacent niche.
2. Record the source, observation date, platform, format, exact opening, performance snapshot, and relevant account baseline when known.
3. Include both strong and weak examples when possible to reduce survivorship bias.
4. Treat likes or views as context, not proof that the hook caused performance. Do not use a fixed popularity threshold as a universal rule.

Maintain two conceptual banks:

- **Pattern bank:** abstract sentence and attention structures.
- **Audience-language bank:** specific words expressing a pain, desire, identity, tension, objection, or aspiration.

## Abstract without copying

For each source hook, identify:

- target viewer;
- structural pattern;
- emotional or identity trigger;
- specificity or proof;
- novelty or contrast;
- opening visual device;
- promised payoff.

Preserve the mechanism, not the sentence. Replace the subject, phrasing, proof, and example. Reject distinctive or near-verbatim imitation. Never copy another creator's identity, footage, or unsupported claim.

## Generate candidates

Produce 10–15 materially different hooks unless the user requests another count. Distribute them across relevant mechanisms such as:

- pain or problem recognition;
- credible result or proof;
- contrarian belief;
- identity or self-recognition;
- specific number, situation, or timeframe;
- open loop with a concrete payoff;
- objection handling;
- demonstration or before-and-after contrast;
- unusual but understandable juxtaposition.

Do not submit synonym swaps as distinct concepts. Front-load the subject, tension, or outcome. Prefer conversational language that is clear on one hearing.

## Maximize the strongest hooks

Revise for:

1. **Clarity:** make the subject and stakes immediately understandable.
2. **Relevance:** make the intended viewer recognize that the video is for them.
3. **Brevity:** remove setup, filler, and written-sounding clauses.
4. **Specificity:** add only details that improve meaning and can be substantiated.
5. **Emotional resonance:** use audience-native language without shame, harassment, stereotyping, or fearmongering.
6. **Novelty:** create a pattern interrupt without sacrificing comprehension or credibility.
7. **Speakability:** read the line aloud five times and repair awkward rhythm or ambiguity.
8. **Promise alignment:** ensure the content fulfills the exact opening claim.
9. **Visual reinforcement:** make the opening image or action clarify, prove, or productively contrast the words rather than merely repeat them.
10. **Brand fit:** adapt intensity, humor, and profanity to the user's voice instead of inheriting them from references.

Target one clean spoken sentence in roughly 3–5 seconds unless the chosen format uses a deliberate text sequence.

## Apply evidence and safety gates

- Never invent numbers, outcomes, testimonials, authority, or social proof.
- Mark factual and numerical claims as **supported**, **needs evidence**, or **remove**.
- Never promise virality, reach, revenue, healing, diagnosis, or another outcome outside the creator's control.
- For health, finance, minors, trauma, or other sensitive topics, avoid guarantees, diagnosis, shame, and exploitative fear. Require appropriate substantiation.
- Avoid deceptive curiosity gaps. The body must pay off the opening promptly.
- Respect platform rules, cultural context, and accessibility.

Reject a candidate if credibility or promise alignment is weak, even when it is attention-grabbing.

## Score and select

Score finalists from 1–5 on:

- clarity;
- audience relevance;
- emotional pull;
- meaningful specificity;
- novelty;
- credibility;
- spoken naturalness;
- promise alignment;
- brand fit;
- ethical risk, reverse-scored.

Require at least 4/5 for credibility and promise alignment. Use the scores to expose tradeoffs, not to claim certainty.

## Deliver the hook package

Return the top three ranked hooks by default. For each include:

- spoken line;
- concise on-screen text;
- opening visual or action;
- first follow-up sentence or body beat;
- why it may work;
- evidence status and risk note;
- one controlled test variant when useful.

Briefly identify the strongest recommendation. Label every prediction as a hypothesis.

## Create a test loop

When the user will publish or provides analytics:

1. Change one meaningful variable per variant when possible.
2. Choose a KPI appropriate to the goal: 3-second hold or scroll-stop plus downstream retention, completion, qualified engagement, click-through, or conversion.
3. Compare against the creator's baseline and use an adequate observation window.
4. Record platform, format, audience, date, distribution context, hook, result, and next hypothesis.
5. Distinguish correlation from causation. Recommend the next test rather than declaring why one hook won.

Cap revision rounds so optimization does not prevent publishing and learning.
